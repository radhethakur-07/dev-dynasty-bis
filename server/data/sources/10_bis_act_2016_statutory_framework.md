# Statutory Framework & Enforcement under Bureau of Indian Standards Act, 2016

## Legal Mandate of the Bureau (BIS)
The Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) is the National Standards Body of India established under the Bureau of Indian Standards Act, 2016 (Act No. 11 of 2016), replacing the predecessor 1986 Act.

## Key Statutory Provisions & Penalties

### Section 16 — Mandatory Use of Standard Mark (QCOs)
The Central Government, if satisfied that it is necessary or expedient so to do in the public interest or for the protection of human, animal or plant health, safety of the environment, or prevention of unfair trade practices, or national security, may direct that any goods or article of any specified industry, process, system or service shall conform to the Indian Standard and direct the use of the Standard Mark under a license or certificate of conformity.

### Section 17 — Prohibition on Improper Use of Standard Mark
No person shall use in relation to any article, goods, process, system or service, or in any title, trade mark or design the Standard Mark or any colorable imitation thereof, except under a valid licence or certificate of conformity granted by the Bureau.

### Section 29 — Strict Criminal & Civil Penalties for Violation
1. Violation of Section 16 (manufacturing, importing, storing, selling, or offering for sale non-compliant mandatory goods):
   - **Imprisonment**: Term which may extend up to two (2) years.
   - **Monetary Fine**: Fine which shall not be less than ₹2,00,000 (two lakh rupees) and which may extend up to ₹5,00,000 (five lakh rupees) for the first violation, or up to ten (10) times the value of the goods produced, sold, or offered for sale.
   - For second or subsequent offenses: Fine not less than ₹5,00,000 and imprisonment up to two years.
2. Violation of Section 17 (unauthorized use of ISI Mark or false hallmarking):
   - Imprisonment up to one year and fine not less than ₹1,00,000 up to ₹5,00,000.

### Section 30 — Offenses by Companies
Where an offense under this Act has been committed by a company, every person who at the time the offense was committed was in charge of, and was responsible to, the company for the conduct of the business of the company, as well as the company itself, shall be deemed guilty of the offense and liable to be proceeded against.

### Section 49 — Consumer Redressal & Statutory Compensation
Where an article marked with the Standard Mark does not conform to the relevant Indian Standard, the consumer or purchaser is entitled to:
1. Replacement of the non-conforming article with a conforming one.
2. Refund of the price paid.
3. In case of precious metal articles (hallmarked gold/silver): If the purity is found below the declared fineness grade, the consumer is legally entitled to compensation from the hallmarked jeweller equal to **two times (2x) the difference in purity value** calculated at the prevailing market price of the metal on the date of purchase.

## Licensing Procedures & MSME Concessions
1. **Normal Procedure (Option 1)**: Factory audit by BIS inspecting officer followed by drawing of sample for testing in an independent laboratory. Licence granted within 60-90 days upon conformity.
2. **Simplified Procedure (Option 2)**: For domestic manufacturers where an in-house laboratory is established and complete test report from a BIS-recognized/NABL-accredited laboratory is submitted. Licence granted within 30 days after initial verification.
3. **MSME & Start-up Concessions**:
   - Micro enterprises: 20% concession on minimum marking fee.
   - Small enterprises: 20% concession on minimum marking fee.
   - Women entrepreneurs & Recognized Start-ups: 50% concession on minimum marking fee for the grant of initial license.
