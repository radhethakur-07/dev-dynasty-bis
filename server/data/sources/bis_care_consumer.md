# BIS Care App & Consumer Verification Guide

Source status: Official BIS Public Information.
Official source URL: https://www.bis.gov.in/bis-apps/?lang=en
Consumer Protection Portal: https://www.bis.gov.in/hallmarking-overview/consumer-protection/?lang=en

## BIS Care Mobile App Capabilities

The official 'BIS CARE' mobile app (available on Google Play Store and Apple App Store) provides essential citizen-centric transparency and verification features:
1. Verify Licence Details (CM/L): Enter the 7-digit or 8-digit CM/L license number displayed on any ISI-marked product to check validity, manufacturer name, brand name, address, and applicable Indian Standard.
2. Verify Registration Details (CRS): Enter the 'R-XXXXXXXX' registration number on electronic and IT goods to check validity and manufacturing details.
3. Verify HUID: Enter the 6-digit alphanumeric code laser-inscribed on hallmarked gold and silver jewellery to view jewellery type, purity grade, jeweller name, and hallmarking centre.
4. Know Your Standards: Search Indian Standards, view scope summaries, recent amendments, and gazette notifications.
5. Locate Testing Laboratories: Find nearest BIS recognized laboratories and assaying & hallmarking centres.

## Consumer Grievance & Complaints Mechanism

- Consumers can lodge grievances directly through the BIS Care App or the consumer portal for:
  - Misuse of BIS Standard Mark (ISI mark) without a valid license.
  - Misleading quality claims on uncertified goods.
  - Substandard hallmarking or incorrect purity on jewellery.
  - Shortfall in product performance compared to Indian Standard specifications.
- Complaints can include geo-tagged photos, invoices, and product packaging details for enforcement action by BIS surveillance officers.

## Consumer Sample Testing & Compensation

- Consumers can submit hallmarked jewellery items to any BIS Recognized Assaying and Hallmarking Centre for independent testing upon payment of nominal testing fees.
- If testing shows lower purity than the declared hallmark grade, the jeweller is legally obligated under BIS regulations to pay compensation equal to two times the shortfall calculated on the purchase value of the article.
